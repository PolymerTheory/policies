Photo Atlas — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 October 2026

Photo Atlas helps you see where your photos were taken on a map. It is designed to be local-first: your photos and the information derived from them stay on your device, and the app has no servers, accounts, or analytics of its own.

What the app accesses

  • Photos & videos on your device (optional). If you enable the device photo source and grant permission, the app reads your photos/videos and their embedded metadata (capture date and GPS, if present) to place them on the map. This happens on your device.
  • Your Dropbox files (optional, read-only). If you connect Dropbox, the app reads photo/video files and their metadata from the Dropbox folders you choose, to build thumbnails and determine where each photo was taken. It does not modify or delete your existing Dropbox files.
  • Location from your photos. The app reads GPS coordinates embedded in your photos' metadata and resolves them to countries/cities on your device. The app does not track your live/device location.

What is stored, and where

  • On your device only: photo metadata (file name, date, GPS, derived country/city, and your manual tags and trips) is stored in a private app database. Dropbox access tokens are stored encrypted using the Android Keystore.
  • Optional cloud backup (off by default): if you turn it on, the app uploads a copy of that metadata database to a private folder in your own Dropbox. The backup is encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM with a key derived from a password you set) before it is uploaded, so you will need that password to restore it — it cannot be recovered for you.
  • Crash logs: if the app crashes, a diagnostic log is saved on your device so you can view it in Settings. It is never sent anywhere automatically — it is only shared if you choose to share it (e.g. by email) to report a problem.

Third parties

  • Dropbox — used only if you connect it, to read your files and (optionally) store your encrypted backup in your own account. See Dropbox's Privacy Policy.
  • Google AdMob (optional ads). Supporting the app by watching a rewarded ad is entirely optional. If you do, Google's advertising SDK may process your device's advertising identifier and similar data to serve and measure ads. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland the app shows a consent prompt first, and you can change your choice any time via “Manage ad privacy choices” in Settings. See Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses data.
  • Google Play Billing (optional donations). An optional one-time donation is processed by Google Play. Google handles the payment; the app never sees your payment card or billing details.

What the app does NOT do

  • It has no servers, user accounts, or analytics of its own.
  • Your photos and metadata are never sent to the developer or to any third party other than the services above (and only as described).
  • We do not sell or share your personal data.

Your control

  • Disconnect Dropbox, turn off the device photo source, or delete the local database at any time in Settings.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its on-device data. See Deleting your data for every step.

Deleting your data

Photo Atlas has no accounts and no servers of its own, so your data lives only on your phone and, if you chose, in your own Dropbox. To delete all of it:

  1. On your phone: uninstall Photo Atlas. Android deletes everything the app stored: its database (photo metadata, locations, tags, trips), thumbnails, crash log, settings and the saved Dropbox connection. To clear the data but keep the app, use Settings → Advanced → Danger zone → Delete local database.
  2. Cloud backup (only if you turned it on): in your Dropbox, delete the folder .photoatlas_backup inside your Camera Uploads folder (or whichever folder you chose as your main folder). It holds photo_atlas.db.enc and photo_atlas.db.prev.enc.
  3. Dropbox access: in the app, Settings → Dropbox → Disconnect, or revoke Photo Atlas under Connected apps in your Dropbox account settings.
  4. Advertising data (only if you watched an optional ad): reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Privacy → Ads). Data collected by Google is handled under Google's Privacy Policy.
  5. Anything you sent us, such as a crash report or an email: write to tueftlerapps@gmail.com and we'll delete it within 30 days.

What's deleted: everything listed above. What's kept: nothing on our side, since Photo Atlas never sends your photos or their metadata to us. Your photos themselves are never changed or deleted by the app; they stay on your phone and in your Dropbox.

Children

Photo Atlas is not directed at children under 13, and does not knowingly collect data from them.
